Slide 02 · Core Concept

What is an element?

Core Definition

An element is a pure substance made of one kind of atom. Elements are the building blocks for everything in the world.

Think of elements like different types of building bricks. You can find pure elements all around you—glowing inside street signs, powering phones, and lighting matches.

Neon Signs Ne

Glass tubes filled with pure neon gas glow bright red-orange when electricity passes through.

Match Heads P

Red phosphorus on a matchbox strip ignites easily with friction to start a flame.

Burning Magnesium Mg

Magnesium ribbon burns with a dazzling white light used in flares and sparklers.

Helium Balloons He

Helium is lighter than surrounding air, lifting party and weather balloons upward.

Lithium-Ion Battery Li

Lithium ions shuttle charge back and forth inside rechargeable phone and laptop batteries.

Slide 04 · Particle Arrangements

How Elements Arrange Their Atoms

Even though every element is made of just one kind of atom, those atoms can join up in three very different ways:

Single Atoms

Individual atoms that do not bond to each other—they drift freely apart (e.g., Neon and Helium gas).

Molecules

Small groups of two or more atoms bonded tightly together, separated from other molecules (e.g., Hydrogen H2, Chlorine Cl2).

Lattices

All atoms bonded continuously with neighbouring atoms in a huge regular grid (e.g., Gold, Sodium, Iron, Copper).

Looking ahead: In Lesson 5, you will learn why atomic structure and electronegativity cause some elements to form molecules while others form lattices.

Slide 08 · Summary

Key Ideas Summary

  • Elements: Pure substances made of only one kind of atom—they cannot be broken down into simpler chemical substances.
  • Single Atoms: Individual atoms moving independently without bonding to one another (such as Neon and Helium).
  • Molecules: Small groups of two or more atoms bonded tightly together (such as H2 and Cl2).
  • Lattices: Huge, repeating 3D grids of atoms bonded continuously to their neighbours (such as Gold, Iron, and Sodium).
