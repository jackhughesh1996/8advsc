    Slide 04 · Decoding the Patterns

    Why Are Some Elements Millions of Times Rarer?

    Reveal each key explanation and copy the underlined takeaways into your exercise book.

    Pattern 1 · Light vs. Heavy Elements

    Why Hydrogen & Helium Dominate

    • Step-by-step buildup: Stars must forge heavy nuclei gradually from H and He fuel.
    • Stronger repulsion: Nuclei with more protons (+) repel each other much more strongly, requiring extreme heat and pressure to fuse.
    • Rare cosmic blasts: Elements heavier than Iron only form briefly during violent supernovae and neutron star collisions.
    Pattern 2 · The Oddo–Harkins Rule

    Why Even Atomic Numbers Spike

    • Helium-4 building blocks: Stellar fusion frequently adds ⁴He nuclei (2 protons + 2 neutrons), jumping by +2 protons at a time (C → O → Ne → Mg → Si).
    • Paired stability: Protons and neutrons gain extra nuclear stability when paired up in even numbers.
    • Result: Even-Z elements (C, O, Fe) are far more common than odd neighbors (B, N, Mn).

    Slide 07 · Summary

    Key Ideas Summary

    • Cosmic Origins: The Big Bang made H and He; stars fuse elements up to Iron (Z = 26); supernovae and neutron star mergers create elements heavier than Iron.
    • Why Light Elements Dominate: Heavy elements are built step-by-step and face stronger proton–proton electrostatic repulsion, requiring extreme heat and rare explosions.
    • Oddo–Harkins Rule: Even-Z elements (C, O, Ne, Mg, Si, Fe) are far more abundant than odd neighbors because fusion adds Helium-4 blocks and paired nucleons are extra stable.
    • Binding Energy Curve: Reactions that climb UP toward the ⁵⁶Fe peak—light fusion on the left and heavy fission on the right—release energy via E = mc².
