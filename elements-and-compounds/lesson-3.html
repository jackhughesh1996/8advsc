Slide 04 · Decoding the Patterns

Why Are Some Elements Millions of Times Rarer?

Reveal each key explanation and copy the underlined takeaways into your exercise book.

Pattern 1 · Light vs. Heavy Elements

Why Hydrogen & Helium Dominate

  • A head start: The early universe already contained mostly hydrogen and helium. Stars later built heavier nuclei from lighter ones.
  • Stronger repulsion: Nuclei with more protons (+) repel each other much more strongly, requiring extreme heat and pressure to fuse.
  • Neutron capture: Some heavier nuclei form gradually in ageing stars. Others form rapidly in events such as neutron-star mergers.
Pattern 2 · The Oddo–Harkins Rule

Why Even Atomic Numbers Spike

  • Helium-4 building blocks: Some stellar fusion reactions add ⁴He nuclei (2 protons + 2 neutrons). For example, adding ⁴He to carbon (6 protons) makes oxygen (8 protons).
  • Paired stability: Protons and neutrons gain extra nuclear stability when paired up in even numbers.
  • Result: Many even-Z elements, such as oxygen and iron, are more abundant than their odd-Z neighbours.

Slide 07 · Summary

Key Ideas Summary

  • Cosmic Origins: The early universe produced mostly H and He. Fusion in stars makes elements such as C and O. Neutron capture can build nuclei heavier than iron.
  • Why Light Elements Dominate: Hydrogen and helium were already abundant before stars began making heavier nuclei.
  • Oddo–Harkins Rule: Even-Z elements often form peaks. Adding helium-4 in some fusion reactions raises the atomic number by two, which helps explain the pattern.
  • Binding Energy Curve: Reactions that climb UP toward the ⁵⁶Fe peak—light fusion on the left and heavy fission on the right—release energy via E = mc².
