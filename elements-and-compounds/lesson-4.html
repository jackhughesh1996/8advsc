Slide 02 · Reading the Periodic Table

Groups (Columns) vs. Periods (Rows)

An element’s coordinates on the Periodic Table reveal its electron shell structure at a glance.

Vertical Columns · Groups

Groups = Valence Electrons

  • Same outer shell count: Main-group elements in the same column have the same number of outer-shell (valence) electrons.
  • Similar behaviour: Because valence electrons drive chemical reactions, elements in a Group share similar properties.
  • Example (Group 1): Li (2, 1), Na (2, 8, 1), and K (2, 8, 8, 1) all have 1 valence electron.
Horizontal Rows · Periods

Periods = Number of Electron Shells

  • Shell counter: Moving down each row adds a brand-new electron shell around the atom.
  • Period 1 (H, He): 1 electron shell.
  • Period 2 (Li → Ne): 2 electron shells.
  • Period 3 (Na → Ar): 3 electron shells.
  • Period 4 (K, Ca…): 4 electron shells.

Slide 04 · Linking Back to Lesson 2 Forces

Why Do These Periodic Trends Happen?

Every trend comes back to two rules from Lesson 2: proton electrostatic pull (+) and distance of electron shells (−).

Atomic Radius

Shrinks Across · Grows Down

  • Across a Period: More protons (+) pull the same shell inward more tightly → radius shrinks.
  • Down a Group: Each row adds a brand-new outer shell farther from the nucleus → radius grows.
Electronegativity

Peaks Up & Right (F = 3.98)

  • Small + high nuclear pull: Small non-metals near the top right (F, O, Cl) pull bonding electrons hardest.
  • Noble gases excluded: Group 18 already has full outer shells and does not attract extra electrons.
Reactivity

Opposite Ends, Opposite Directions

  • Group 1 (lose e⁻): More reactive going down—outer electron is farther away and easier to lose.
  • Group 17 (gain e⁻): More reactive going up—smaller atoms pull an extra electron more strongly.

Slide 06 · Real-World Reactivity

Why Do Sodium (Na) & Potassium (K) React Similarly—Yet Differently?

Sodium (Na · Z = 11) Shells: 2, 8, 1
Why they react similarly: Both sit in Group 1 and have 1 valence electron, so both readily lose that outer electron to form +1 ions in water.
Potassium (K · Z = 19) Shells: 2, 8, 8, 1
Why K reacts more violently: Potassium has 4 shells instead of 3, so its outer electron is farther from the nucleus, feels weaker electrostatic attraction, and is lost much faster.
Slide 07 · Summary

Key Ideas Summary

  • Groups & Periods: Columns (Groups) give valence electrons and similar reactivity; rows (Periods) give the number of electron shells.
  • Atomic Radius: Decreases left-to-right across a Period (stronger nuclear pull on the same shell) and increases down a Group (new shells added).
  • Electronegativity: Increases up and right toward Fluorine (3.98); Noble Gases are excluded because their outer shells are already full.
  • Reactivity: Alkali metals (Group 1) get more reactive down the group (outer e⁻ farther away); Halogens (Group 17) get more reactive up the group (smaller atoms pull e⁻ harder).
