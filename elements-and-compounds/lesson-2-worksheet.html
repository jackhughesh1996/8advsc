Part C. What Holds the Atom Together?

Use key terms from the word bank in your explanations.
Word Bank: electrostatic attraction · opposite charges · repel · strong nuclear force · neutrons · buffer

1. Electrons in Shells: Explain what keeps negatively charged electrons bound inside an atom instead of flying away.

Hint: Name the force and describe the attraction between protons and electrons.

Model Answer: Opposite charges attract via the electrostatic force: positively charged protons in the nucleus pull inward on negatively charged electrons, holding them in shells around the nucleus.

2. Inside the Nucleus: A Helium-4 nucleus (2 p⁺, 2 n⁰) is stable, whereas a 2-proton nucleus with zero neutrons (²He) breaks apart immediately. Explain why, naming the nuclear force and the role of neutrons.

Model Answer: Positively charged protons repel each other electrostatically. The short-range strong nuclear force acts as nuclear glue to bind the nucleus, and neutrons act as buffers that add strong nuclear attraction without adding extra positive repulsion.

Part E. Extension Challenge: Atom vs. Ion (Sodium, Z = 11)

Stretch question

A neutral Sodium atom (Na) loses 1 outer-shell electron during a reaction to form a Sodium ion (Na⁺).

(a) Write the electron shell arrangement for both:

Neutral Na: 2, 8, 1 Na⁺ ion: 2, 8

(b) Show why the net charge of Na⁺ is +1 (even though it lost something):

Answer: Na⁺ still has 11 protons (+11) and now has 10 electrons (−10), so Net Charge = 11 − 10 = +1. Losing a negatively charged electron leaves an excess positive charge.

(c) Neon (Z = 10) also has 10 electrons (2, 8). Why is Na⁺ still Sodium and not Neon?

Answer: Element identity depends only on the number of protons (Atomic Number Z). Na⁺ still has 11 protons, whereas Neon has 10 protons.
