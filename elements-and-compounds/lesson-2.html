Atomic Accounting Rules

Atomic Number (Z) = number of protons (defines the element). Mass Number (A) = protons + neutrons (electrons have negligible mass). Net Charge = protons − electrons (neutral atoms have equal p⁺ and e⁻).

Slide 05 · Electron Shells

Electron Shell Filling Pattern

Electrons fill from the inside out—matching the rows (Periods) of the Periodic Table.

Filling Sequence Across the Table
2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32
Period (Row) Electrons Added
Period 12
Period 2 & Period 38, then 8
Period 4 & Period 518, then 18
Period 6 & Period 732, then 32
First 20 Elements: 2, 8, 8, 2

For elements 1–20 (Hydrogen to Calcium), electrons simply fill in order:

2, 8, 8, 2

Example: Potassium (19) = 2, 8, 8, 1 · Calcium (20) = 2, 8, 8, 2

Bonus Note

From Period 4 onward, the shell being filled does not always match the period number—extra electrons drop back to finish inner shells.

Slide 07 · Atomic Stability

What Holds the Atom Together?

1. Electrostatic Force

Attraction & Repulsion
  • Opposite charges attract: + protons pull − electrons into shells.
  • Like charges repel: + protons push each other apart.

2. Strong Nuclear Force

Nuclear Glue
  • Short-range glue holding protons & neutrons together.
  • Neutrons act as buffers to help overcome proton repulsion.
Slide 08 · Worksheet Solutions (Q1–Q5)

Worked Problem Set

Review each worksheet question on screen—press → or ↓ to reveal each worked answer step by step.

Q1. A Neon atom has 9 neutrons and 10 electrons.

(a) How many protons does it have? 10 protons (Z = 10)
(b) What is its mass number? 19 (10 p⁺ + 9 n⁰ · isotope ¹⁹Ne)
(c) What is its overall charge? 0 (10 p⁺ − 10 e⁻)

Q2. Predicting net charge when electrons change:

(a) Neutral Neon gains 1 electron. Net charge? −1 (10 p⁺ − 11 e⁻)
(b) Neutral Sodium (11 p⁺) loses 1 electron. Net charge? +1 (11 p⁺ − 10 e⁻)

Q3. An unknown atom has 17 protons and 18 neutrons.

(a) Identify the element: Chlorine (Cl)
(b) What is its mass number? 35 (17 + 18)
(c) Electron shell arrangement: 2, 8, 7

Q4. Explain what keeps electrons bound inside an atom.

Opposite charges attract via the electrostatic force: positively charged protons in the nucleus pull inward on negatively charged electrons, holding them in shells around the nucleus.

Q5. Explain what holds the nucleus together despite proton–proton repulsion, including the role of neutrons.

Protons repel each other electrostatically, but the short-range strong nuclear force acts as glue holding the nucleus together. Neutrons act as buffers to help overcome proton repulsion.
Slide 09 · Summary

Key Ideas Summary

  • Subatomic Particles: Protons (+1, 1 amu) and neutrons (0, 1 amu) form the dense nucleus; tiny electrons (−1, ~1/1836 amu) occupy shells.
  • Shell Pattern: Electrons follow the 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32 pattern across Periods 1–7—starting with 2, 8, 8, 2 for elements 1–20.
  • Forces in the Atom: Electrostatic attraction (+ ↔ −) holds electrons in shells; strong nuclear glue and neutron buffers overcome proton repulsion in the nucleus.
  • Atomic Accounting: Atomic Number (Z) = protons; Mass Number (A) = protons + neutrons; Net Charge = protons − electrons.
